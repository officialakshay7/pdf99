How to Sign a PDF Online — Free Methods That Actually Work

July 14, 2026 5 min read By PDF99 Team

Signing a PDF used to require printing it, signing it with a pen, and either scanning it back or faxing it. In 2026, the whole process can happen entirely online in a few minutes — legally and for free. Here's how to do it without paying for Adobe Acrobat or a dedicated e-signature service.

What counts as a valid electronic signature?

Before the methods, it's worth knowing what's legally acceptable. In most countries, an electronic signature is legally equivalent to a handwritten one for most document types. The key legislation: the US ESIGN Act, the EU eIDAS regulation, and the UK Electronic Communications Act all recognise electronic signatures as legally binding for standard commercial and personal contracts.

The exceptions are narrow: some jurisdictions require wet signatures for wills, court filings in certain cases, and some property transactions. For everyday business documents — employment contracts, client agreements, NDAs, purchase orders — an electronic signature is fully valid.

Method 1: Use a browser-based signature tool

Several free online tools let you sign a PDF in your browser without creating an account:

  1. Upload your PDF to the signing tool
  2. Click where you want to add your signature
  3. Draw your signature with a mouse or trackpad, type your name and let the tool style it, or upload a photo of your handwritten signature
  4. Position and resize the signature image on the page
  5. Download the signed PDF

PDF99's sign tool is currently in development — when available it will support all three signature creation methods directly in your browser, with no account required.

Method 2: Use Preview on Mac

If you're on a Mac, Preview has excellent built-in signature support that most people don't know about:

  1. Open your PDF in Preview
  2. Click the Markup toolbar button (the pencil icon) to show the markup tools
  3. Click the signature button (looks like a cursive signature)
  4. Choose "Create Signature" — you can sign on the trackpad or use your iPhone's camera to photograph a signature on paper
  5. Once created, drag the signature to the correct position on the document
  6. Save the PDF

This is completely free, built into every Mac, and produces a proper PDF with your signature embedded — not just a screenshot. The iPhone camera method is particularly good if you want your actual handwritten signature in the document.

Method 3: Use Adobe's free tier

Adobe offers a limited free tier for signing documents through their Acrobat online service. You get a small number of free signings per month before hitting the paywall. For occasional use — signing a lease once, countersigning a contractor agreement — the free tier is sufficient. Create a free Adobe account, upload your PDF, and use their guided signing workflow.

Method 4: Type your name and take a screenshot

This sounds too simple, but for many informal contexts it's perfectly acceptable. Type your name in the signature field, take a screenshot, and embed that as the signature image. Or simply type your name in italics directly into a PDF annotation box. Courts and businesses have accepted typed names as electronic signatures for over 20 years.

What about contracts that need a "proper" signature?

If the other party to a contract specifically requires a qualified electronic signature (QES) or an advanced electronic signature (AES) — which have higher legal standards and require identity verification — you'll need a certified e-signature platform like DocuSign, HelloSign, or similar. These typically charge per document or per month.

For the vast majority of everyday business documents, a standard electronic signature from any of the free methods above is completely sufficient.

Tips for signing professionally

Use black ink when drawing a signature — it photographs most clearly and looks professional on printed documents. Keep the signature consistent with how you sign physical documents — a wildly different digital signature looks suspicious. If you're signing multiple copies of the same document, use the same signature image or drawing to ensure consistency.
