How to Reduce PDF File Size on iPhone and iPad

July 7, 2026 5 min read By PDF99 Team

Large PDF files cause two specific problems on iPhone and iPad. First, they take a long time to load, especially on older devices or slower connections. Second, many apps and web forms cap file uploads at sizes that a typical scanned PDF will exceed. This guide covers your best options for making PDF files smaller on iOS, starting with the free approaches.

Method 1: Use PDF99 in Safari

The simplest option is to use a browser-based compressor directly in Safari on your iPhone. No app to install, no account to create.

  1. Open Safari and go to pdf99.org/compress-pdf
  2. Tap "Choose PDF file" and select your PDF from Files, iCloud Drive, or from an email attachment
  3. Adjust the quality slider — 75% is a good starting point that typically reduces file size by 40-60% without visible quality loss
  4. Tap "Compress PDF" and wait for the compression to complete (usually 10-30 seconds)
  5. Tap "Download" and choose "Save to Files" to save the compressed version to your device

The whole process works entirely in Safari — your PDF is processed in the browser and never uploaded to a server. This makes it both fast and private.

Method 2: Print to PDF (lossless)

iOS has a built-in "Print to PDF" function that can reduce file size for PDFs that contain redundant metadata, revision history, or inefficient structure:

  1. Open the PDF in Files or any app that can display it
  2. Tap the share icon and choose "Print"
  3. On the print preview screen, pinch outward on the preview page with two fingers — this reveals a full-screen PDF preview
  4. Tap the share icon in the top right corner of the preview
  5. Choose "Save to Files" to save the reprinted version

This method is lossless — image quality is unchanged. It's most effective on PDFs that have accumulated extra data through repeated editing. It won't help much with scanned document PDFs that are large because of their image content.

Method 3: Files app compression (iOS 16+)

From iOS 16 onwards, the Files app includes a native PDF compression option for PDFs stored in iCloud Drive:

  1. Open the Files app and find your PDF
  2. Long-press the PDF file to open the context menu
  3. Tap "Compress" — note this creates a ZIP file of the PDF, not a compressed PDF

This creates a ZIP archive containing the PDF. It's useful for reducing transfer size when you're sending the file directly, but the recipient will need to unzip it before opening. For portal uploads that require a PDF specifically, this method won't work.

Which method should you use?

For most situations — reducing a PDF to upload to a portal, email, or share via WhatsApp — the browser-based compressor in Safari is the best approach. It gives you control over quality, shows you the resulting file size before you download, and produces a properly compressed PDF rather than a ZIP.

The Print to PDF method is worth trying first if your PDF came from a Word document or was created digitally (not scanned) — it's lossless and sometimes surprisingly effective.

If you're dealing with a very large multi-page scanned document, the browser compressor with the quality slider at 50-60% will produce the most significant size reduction.

What size can you realistically achieve?

A typical scanned document page (A4, standard office scan quality) starts at around 300KB to 1MB per page when converted to PDF. With moderate compression at 75% quality, expect to get each page down to 80-200KB. A 10-page scanned document that starts at 6MB will typically compress to 1-2MB. Whether that meets your target depends on the specific upload limit you're working with.
